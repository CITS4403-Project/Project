\section{Model specification}
\label{sec:model}

The model represents the transport network as a graph of entities that carry a
load and fail above a fixed capacity. Entity $i$ has an open or closed status
$s_i(t)$ and a carried load $L_i(t)$, and it interacts with its neighbours
$\mathcal{N}_i$. With the state $x_i(t) = (s_i(t), L_i(t))$, the generic
update rule is
\begin{equation}
  x_i(t+1) = f\bigl(x_i(t), \{x_j(t)\}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i}; \theta\bigr),
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ collects the capacity, tolerance, redistribution and rerouting
parameters. In the transport case $f$ closes every open entity above capacity,
removes the round's overloads together, and forwards their load to surviving
neighbours, following \citet{motter2002cascade} with the local-redistribution
variant below. A round is algorithmic and carries no wall-clock meaning.

\subsection{Components, loads and failure}
The rail layer is an undirected simple tree of 86 mapped stations and 85
verified adjacent segments, frozen for 5 October 2026, 07:00 to 09:00
Australia/Perth. The multilayer experiments split each station into a
persistent terminal $T{:}i$ and a closable rail facility $R{:}i$ with a
one-minute access edge. Rail edges take
$t_e = \max(0.1,\ d_e/(1000\,v)\cdot 60)$ minutes at an assumed $v = 40$ km/h.
Backup edges join terminals with effective times including walking and waiting,
using the four manual pairs in \texttt{backup\_edges.csv} as ground truth and
extending the pool with GTFS candidates; a five-minute transfer joins the Perth
and Perth Underground terminals. Standby buses activate immediately after the
initial closure with unlimited capacity in the baseline scenario, and multilayer
loads are terminal-subset betweenness over the intact terminal-pair denominator.

The initial load of station $i$ is its unnormalised betweenness on the intact
graph, $L0_i = \sum \sigma_{st}(i) / \sigma_{st}$, summed over station pairs
with $s \neq i \neq t$; the ratio is the share of shortest paths through $i$. The values stay unnormalised because the graph shrinks
during a cascade: normalised loads would rise with the shrinking node set and
rounds would not be comparable. The load modes are \texttt{betweenness} as
above; \texttt{betweenness\_freq} with edge length $1/\text{trips}$;
\texttt{betweenness\_plus\_trips} with $0.1\,\text{trips served}_i$ added to
give leaf stations a positive baseline; and \texttt{demand}, the AM-peak
scheduled departure count \texttt{am\_peak\_stops}, a proxy for boardings
rather than observed passengers.

Capacities follow
\begin{equation}
  C_i = (1+\alpha) K_i, \qquad \alpha \ge 0,
  \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
with default reference capacity $K_i = L0_i$, fixed from the intact graph. A
station with $L0_i = 0$ gets $C_i = 0$ and can still fail under positive
redistributed load in the static mode. The demand experiment uses
$K_i = c f_i$, where $f_i = \text{trips\_served}_i$ and
$c = \max_i L0_i / f_i$ is the smallest uniform scaling that keeps
$K_i \ge L0_i$ on the frozen graph; the scale $c$ is a scenario assumption
recorded with the demand results.

At each round every surviving station with $L_i > C_i + \delta$ fails, where
the numerical tolerance $\delta$ defaults to $10^{-12}$; all overloads of a
round are collected before any station is removed, and the cascade stops at the
first clear round. The first failure is an explicit \texttt{target} when one is
named, otherwise the \texttt{load}, \texttt{degree} or \texttt{random} trigger
takes the largest initial load, the highest degree with station-ID ties, or a
uniform seeded draw. A line closure replaces the trigger with one initial
batch, removed before the first check and excluded from the secondary avalanche
size. In the static mode each closed station forwards its load equally over
survivors or in proportion to their capacities, falling back to equal shares
when all have zero; a station with no survivor removes its load, and otherwise
load is conserved. The dynamic mode recomputes loads on
the surviving graph each round and ignores redistribution; on a tree this
cannot raise a survivor's betweenness, so containment reflects dropped journeys
rather than headroom. The containment tolerance $\alpha^*$ is the smallest
sampled $\alpha$ at which the maximum-load trigger causes no secondary failure.

\subsection{Assumptions and initialisation}
All facilities start open; loads and capacities come from the intact graph, and
the trigger or initial batch is applied before the first overload check. The
model assumes undirected adjacent links, scheduled services as load proxies,
hop distances for load concentration, minutes only in the multilayer layer,
symmetric bus times, unlimited immediate standby, and one service date. An
empty initial batch closes nothing; an
$\alpha$ of zero adds no headroom and can fail a station immediately; duplicate
backup endpoints keep the fastest connection; zero capacity does not exempt a
station from overload checks.

The defaults in \texttt{src/transperth/config.py} are $\alpha = 0.2$, master
seed 0, tolerance $10^{-12}$, and the 81-point grid
$\alpha = 0, 0.025, \ldots, 2$. The tolerance only guards floating-point
comparisons. The grid starts at zero and reaches twice the $L0$ reference; it
brackets the sampled containment tolerances near 0.5 and 0.75, and the 0.025
step resolves the abrupt static transition, as the uncertainty experiment
confirms.
